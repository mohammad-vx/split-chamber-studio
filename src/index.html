    How a video is made, checked, accepted or rejected

    1. Design. The pattern you chose (or a random one) builds a machine from the video's seed: part sizes, speeds, tempo, gravity, colours. The same seed always gives the same video.
    2. Simulate. The physics runs in the background, frame by frame (no picture yet). Every part is solid: balls collide with it, and a ball squeezed between a moving part and a wall or another part always splits. The only thing balls pass through is a glowing laser, which splits what it touches. The finale starts when the ball count you chose in Stop at is reached (or the arena is full), or when the time for the chosen Length runs out.
    3. Simulation checks (about 3 s, cheap). Rejected immediately if: physics unstable · a ball passed through a solid part · no ending · length outside the pattern's range · first split later than 3 s · climax too small · scene too empty at half time.
    4. Soundtrack and preview. Music and impact sounds are generated in sync with the frames, and a small 540×960 preview is rendered.
    5. Preview checks on the real file: loudness −17…−13 LUFS · true peak ≤ −1 dBTP · no silence over 1.5 s · no frozen or black picture over 1 s · not too similar to a video you already accepted.
    6. Waiting for review. A video that passes every check gets a score (base 50 + early hook + comfortable length + loudness + novelty) and waits for you. Nothing is accepted without you, unless you turned on Auto-accept score ≥ N.
    7. You decide. Accept renders the full 1080×1920 · 60 fps master, re-runs the simulation to prove every frame is identical, and checks picture quality (SSIM) and loudness again. Reject deletes the media and keeps a small record with your reason.
